# Apply & evolve — From principle to the next sentence

The system is useful only when it helps someone make a better decision.

You do not need to reread every chapter before writing a button or a slide.
You do need to know who is reading, what is happening around them, and what the
communication must help them understand or do.

## Choose one of two manuals

Use [Marketing](../ux-voice/marketing.md) when the communication helps someone:

- recognize an operating problem;
- understand why it persists;
- evaluate Singular's approach;
- trust a claim or piece of evidence;
- choose a commercial next step.

Use [Product](../ux-voice/product.md) when the communication helps someone:

- understand or change a state;
- review scope, evidence, or consequence;
- authorize, accept, publish, or recover;
- understand permissions or limitations;
- interpret AI behavior.

The channel does not make the choice. A release deck can use Product writing.
A case study uses Marketing writing even when it describes a product. A
website error uses Product writing because the person needs to recover, not be
persuaded.

## Apply the system in five moves

### 1. Name the moment

Who is here? What just happened? What are they trying to decide? What could go
wrong if the message is vague?

### 2. Start from the client reality

Find the specific pain or need in [Singular foundations](./05-experience-foundations.md).
Do not begin with a preferred phrase, component, or AI capability.

### 3. Choose the manual

Marketing creates recognition and confidence. Product creates understanding
and control. If a piece tries to do both, decide which job is primary.

### 4. Write from the foundation

Make the main point immediate. Carry the context that changes interpretation.
Name ownership and commitments. Put evidence before assertion. Keep meaningful
human control visible.

### 5. Read it as the person

Can they explain what this means, what is true, and what happens next? If not,
the copy is not finished.

## Trace a material decision

Most copy does not need a case file. Record the reasoning when a choice changes
shared meaning, communicates a consequential state, or affects more than one
surface.

```markdown
## Decision

Context:
Person and job:
Pain or risk:
Evidence:
Foundation:
Decision and trade-off:
System owner:
Application:
Validation:
```

### Worked decision: product approval

**Context:** A client approver reviews proposed Sprint scope on mobile before
work begins.

**Person and job:** The approver must authorize selected work without
reconstructing the full project.

**Pain or risk:** A generic “Approve” action hides scope, commitment, excluded
work, and the resulting state.

**Evidence:** The current product distinguishes Pre-Work authorization,
selected Tasks, Story Points, identity confirmation, and Post-Work acceptance.

**Foundations:** Preserve relevant context. Make commitments explicit. Keep
meaningful human control.

**Decision:** Show phase, selected scope, total SP, project context,
consequence, and approver identity before the final action. Label the action
**Authorize scope**. Mobile can reduce supporting detail, but not decision
information.

**Ownership:** The Design System owns hierarchy and interaction patterns. The
product owns selection, permission, identity, and the state transition.

**Validation:** The approver can explain what is included, what changes after
authorization, and what remains proposed.

This is how a client pain becomes a foundation, a Design System rule, and two
words on a button.

## Evidence appendix

Keep evidence honest. A label should tell the reader how much trust to place in
the information—not make weak evidence look stronger.

### Evidence labels

- **Documented:** Explicit in approved direction, product behavior, traceable
  customer evidence, or a Design System contract.
- **Inferred:** Consistently suggested by product structure, flows, or aligned
  indirect sources.
- **Needs validation:** A useful hypothesis with insufficient evidence.

Documented does not automatically mean customer-validated. An inference can
guide reversible work. It cannot become a customer quote.

### Claim status

- **Verified:** Source, method, scope, period, permission, and wording are
  approved.
- **Provisional:** At least one publication requirement is missing.
- **Retired:** The claim expired, was contradicted, or is no longer approved.

Historical promises such as 10 business days, under 45 days, 75%, 90%, 40%,
pricing, ROI, testimonials, and client logos remain provisional until the
source, method, scope, and publication permission are recorded.

### What we know and what we still need to learn

Documented direction includes the USD 5M–20M central ICP, established operating
workflows, founder/owner/operator audience, client ownership, current product
roles and naming, and the shared blue/cyan identity.

Direct research is still needed for pain frequency in customer language,
industry differences, Business Champion authority, buying committees, adoption
barriers, approval information needs, and comprehension of Company Brain,
Rivers, and Totems.

## Let evidence change the system

When new evidence changes the story:

1. update the upstream foundation once;
2. review what changes in Marketing, Product, and the Design System;
3. keep domain behavior in the product repository;
4. record the material decision when others will need the reasoning;
5. verify the source, links, skill package, and published page.

When sources disagree, current product behavior wins for what the product does;
approved business direction wins for positioning; customer research wins for
the experience it studied. Historical material is evidence, not automatic
authority.

The point is not to freeze a perfect voice. It is to keep Singular coherent
while the business, products, and evidence get better.
