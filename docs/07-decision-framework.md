# Decision framework

**Status:** v0.1 working reference

**Purpose:** Make every material Singular decision traceable from client reality
to an applied rule

A Singular decision is not “on brand” because it resembles an existing screen
or uses the right color. It is on system when its rationale can be followed
from user and problem to foundation, layer, and concrete expression.

## The decision chain

```text
client situation
→ user and current job
→ pain, risk, or decision
→ evidence level
→ experience foundation
→ design or content decision
→ system owner and layer
→ surface expression
→ validation or measurement
```

Use the full chain for consequential or shared decisions. A narrow mechanical
application of an existing rule can cite the rule without recreating the full
rationale.

## Decision record template

```markdown
## Decision

### Context
What client or product situation triggered this?

### User and job
Who is acting, and what are they trying to understand, decide, or do?

### Pain or risk
What ambiguity, cost, dependency, or trust failure must be reduced?

### Evidence
Documented, inferred, or needs validation. Cite the source.

### Foundation
Which experience foundation governs the decision?

### Decision
State the rule in one sentence.

### Why
Explain how the decision supports the job and reduces the risk.

### Trade-off accepted
What becomes less flexible, less compact, slower, or more complex?

### System layer and owner
Brand & Voice, DS foundation, platform, surface, domain, or instance.

### Application
Show where and how the rule appears.

### Validation
What evidence would confirm, revise, or retire the decision?
```

| Client owns the resulting assets | Buyer wants expertise without a new dependency | Ownership and commitments | Reject “we own the systems”; make ownership explicit |

## Worked decision 1: Website hero

### Context

An established operator arrives with operating friction but may frame the
interest as “we need AI.”

### User and job

Founder, owner, or operator deciding whether Singular understands the business
problem and offers a credible next step.

### Pain or risk

A tool-first hero sounds like every AI vendor. A pain-only hero creates
recognition without a desirable future.

### Evidence

Documented positioning direction; direct audience response still needs
validation.

### Foundation

Start from operating reality. Express calm authority.

### Decision

Lead the hero with the desired operating future; use supporting copy and the
body to demonstrate the operating obstacle and mechanism.

### Trade-off accepted

The hero will not explain the full architecture or list every pain.

### System layer

Marketing Voice & Tone plus website surface.

### Application

- Headline: one operating future.
- Subheadline: audience, obstacle, approach, and relevant boundary.
- Body: pain → consequence → operating model → Singular approach → evidence →
  next step.

### Validation

Test whether target buyers can explain who Singular is for, what problem it
addresses, and what the next step is without prompting.

## Worked decision 2: Product approval

### Context

A mobile executive approver authorizes Pre-Work or accepts QA-passed
Post-Work.

### User and job

Make a consequential decision quickly while understanding selected scope,
evidence, and resulting state.

### Pain or risk

“Approve all” and biometric confirmation can create a false sense that scope
was reviewed.

### Evidence

Documented in the iOS product flow.

### Foundation

Preserve context. Make commitments explicit. Keep meaningful human control.

### Decision

Show phase, selected Tasks, total SP, decision context, exclusions, and
consequence before the final signature.

### Trade-off accepted

The flow requires one review step before Face ID and cannot be reduced to a
single tap.

### System layer

Product voice, iOS surface, and product-domain approval policy.

### Application

- Pre-Work uses authorization language.
- Post-Work uses acceptance language.
- The user can deselect Tasks before signing.
- Success copy names the resulting state.

### Validation

Confirm that users can identify what they approved, what phase it belongs to,
and what happens next.

## Worked decision 3: AI result state

### Context

Studio agents prepare a proposed product change in a sandbox or local preview.

### User and job

The Singularity operator must decide whether to review, revise, hand off, or
continue.

### Pain or risk

An animated completion state or “Done” message can be mistaken for a production
change.

### Evidence

Documented Studio behavior and business rules.

### Foundation

Use evidence before assertion. Keep automation under human control.

### Decision

Call the result a preview until an external destination confirms publication.
Keep Preview, Diff, Sources, and the next decision visible.

### Trade-off accepted

Copy is more qualified than a celebratory “Your change is live.”

### System layer

Product voice, Studio surface, and product-domain run state.

### Application

> The preview and diff are ready for review. No production publish has been
> confirmed.

### Validation

Users must correctly distinguish a proposal, a sandbox result, and a confirmed
external publication.

## Worked decision 4: Semantic status

### Context

Operational products show many states alongside the Singular brand.

### User and job

Client, Talent, QA, Manager, or Admin needs to scan state and decide what
requires action.

### Pain or risk

If blue or cyan means both “Singular” and “approved,” color becomes ambiguous.
Color-only status is also inaccessible.

### Evidence

Documented cross-product need and accessibility requirement.

### Foundation

Make complexity navigable. Use evidence before assertion. Accessibility is
foundational.

### Decision

Keep brand, interaction, and semantic status roles separate. Pair status color
with an explicit label and relevant evidence or action.

### Trade-off accepted

The palette has more semantic roles than a purely monochromatic brand system.

### System layer

DS foundation and surface/domain mapping.

### Validation

Status remains understandable without color and does not conflict with the
primary interaction role.

## Risk surface map

| Risk | Failure point | System mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Generic “AI company” story | Context disappears before positioning | Start with ICP, jobs, and operating problem |
| Documentation becomes repetitive | User definitions drift across files | Canonical source map and explicit downstream summaries |
| Marketing language enters product | Persuasion replaces state clarity | Intent router and separate Marketing/Product chapters |
| Product behavior leaks into DS | Shared components acquire permissions or domain logic | Foundation/platform/surface/domain ownership |
| Visual rationale is invented as research | Brand preference is described as a customer finding | Evidence labels and explicit brand-convention boundary |
| Claims become truth through repetition | Provisional metrics are reused without source | Claim status and publication gate |
| Agents overstate completion | Local success is described as live | External confirmation requirement |
| Automation reduces control | Scope or consequence is hidden | Review, source trace, human gate, and recovery |
| Naming fragments | Story, Task, Agile, and Stories appear as separate systems | Canonical terminology and surface-specific mapping |

## Scalability check

As Singular grows, ambiguity will increase around:

- industry-specific needs;
- multiple buyer committees;
- localized language;
- new agent and product states;
- product-specific naming;
- claim ownership and expiry;
- when a local pattern becomes shared;
- who can change a foundation.

Systemize next only when there is real independent ownership or update
frequency. Do not split a document merely because it is long. Do split when a
section has a different owner, evidence lifecycle, or audience that causes
repeated review friction.

## Decision quality gate

Before a material decision is approved:

- [ ] The user and current job are named.
- [ ] The pain, risk, or decision is specific.
- [ ] Evidence status is visible.
- [ ] A foundation supports the choice.
- [ ] The decision and trade-off are explicit.
- [ ] The correct system layer owns it.
- [ ] Product-domain logic remains in the product.
- [ ] Marketing and Product intent are not mixed.
- [ ] Accessibility and states are considered.
- [ ] The validation or learning path is named.
