# Marketing voice & tone

**Use for:** Website, presentations, proposals, case studies, social, marketing
email, and public Design System narrative

**Owner:** Product Design; reviewed by Marketing

Marketing helps the right buyer recognize an operating problem, understand
Singular's approach, distinguish proof from promise, and choose a useful next
step. It applies [Client reality](../docs/01-client-context.md) and
[Brand & core voice](../brand/README.md) without redefining them.

## The reader and the decision

Write primarily for a founder, owner, executive operator, or Business Champion
inside an established US SMB.

They are not deciding whether AI is interesting. They are deciding whether one
important workflow can create leverage without reducing control, disrupting the
team, or creating another dependency.

Marketing must answer:

1. Do you understand the operating problem?
2. What changes, and why is Singular the right partner?
3. What is proven, provisional, or unknown?
4. What is the next commitment?

Strong fit requires a consequential recurring workflow, an accountable owner,
a visible consequence, willingness to change how work happens, and a way to
evaluate the result. Fit is weak when the request is only for a demo, no one
owns the workflow, or success cannot be defined.

## Positioning

Singular is an outcome partner that turns one consequential workflow into a
governed operating capability the client can own and adopt.

Use these boundaries:

- start with operating pain, not an AI category;
- describe changed work, not disconnected tools or automations;
- include context, ownership, review, and adoption in the mechanism;
- make client ownership and meaningful human judgment explicit.

Avoid positioning Singular as an AI consultancy, tool shop, chatbot vendor, or
partner that owns the client's systems.

Company Brain, Rivers, and Totems are optional explanations:

- **Rivers:** changing operating signals.
- **Totems:** stable rules, permissions, definitions, and institutional
  knowledge.
- **Company Brain:** context that lets people and agents interpret live signals
  through stable rules.

Define the operating idea before the metaphor. Do not use these terms when they
add another concept the reader must learn.

## Narrative

Use this sequence when it helps the buyer decide:

```text
operating future or recognizable pain
→ business consequence
→ why the problem persists
→ changed operating model
→ Singular's role
→ evidence or constraint
→ next step
```

The hero may lead with the desired future. The body must prove that Singular
understands the obstacle before introducing Company Brain, agents, platforms,
or tools.

### Example

**Hero**

> Turn the business you're running into the business that runs itself.

**Support**

> Growth has spread context and decisions across people and tools. Singular
> turns one consequential workflow into a governed capability your team can
> understand, use, and own.

The hero is intentionally ambitious. The support gives it an operating
boundary. Do not add a number merely to make it sound concrete.

## Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | Calm, declarative, ambitious | Make the operating future visible |
| Problem | Specific, empathetic | Create recognition without dramatizing |
| Mechanism | Precise, plain | Explain how work changes |
| Outcome | Concrete, evidence-led | Show what becomes different |
| Proof | Factual, restrained | Support trust |
| CTA | Direct, low-friction | Name the next step |
| Presentation | Executive, structured | Move from context to decision |
| Social or email | Immediate, human | Earn attention without clickbait |

## Message patterns

### Headlines and supporting copy

A headline communicates one idea in the buyer's operating language. It remains
meaningful without the visual and avoids abstract category language.

- Weak: “Unlock transformative AI-powered growth.”
- Singular: “Turn operational drag into a system your team can run.”

Supporting copy adds the audience, problem, mechanism, or boundary; it does not
repeat the headline.

Contrast—“Not X. Y.”—is optional. Use it to correct a false category, not as a
signature in every section.

### Calls to action

Name the actual next step:

- “Map the first workflow”
- “Start the AI audit”
- “Book a strategy call”
- “Read the case study”

Avoid “Learn more,” “Get started,” “Submit,” or “Discover the future” when the
next action is known. Verify the current commercial path before publishing.

## Surface guidance

### Presentations and proposals

- Use one decision or claim per slide.
- State the takeaway in the title.
- Separate current state, proposed change, evidence, risk, and decision.
- End decision slides with the exact approval or next step.

- Weak title: “Automation opportunities”
- Singular title: “Manual follow-up is delaying renewals and hiding account
  risk.”

### Case studies, social, and email

- **Case study:** client context → problem → constraint → change → evidence →
  outcome and boundary.
- **Social:** one recognizable idea without removing the claim boundary.
- **Email:** why the message matters and the next action, without manufactured
  urgency or artificial enthusiasm.

## Claims and evidence

Use the canonical [evidence appendix](../docs/08-application-map.md#evidence-appendix)
before publishing a metric, speed, pricing, ROI, testimonial, logo, or customer
result. Never invent or reconstruct proof. Terms such as scalable, faster,
intelligent, AI-native, or automation require a defined behavior or comparison.

## Marketing review

- Are the buyer, problem, and consequence recognizable before the tool?
- Does the mechanism explain changed work, ownership, and review?
- Are the CTA and evidence boundary explicit?
- Is the language calm, specific, and unmistakably Singular?
